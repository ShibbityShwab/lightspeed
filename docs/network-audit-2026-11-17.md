## 1. What was measured, and on which leg

Three measurements are kept separate on purpose; mixing them has burned this
repo before (see `docs/server-leg-signal.md`, WF-063).

| # | Quantity | Family / leg | Estimator and sample size |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Fleet health, traffic, drop reasons | relay-local counters read over HTTP `/health` and `/metrics` on `:8080`; relay process lifetime | one snapshot per relay, 8 relays (n=8), captured 00:38:35–00:38:43Z |
| B | **M1 drain** — rejected ALPN handshakes | QUIC control plane, relay-local counter `lightspeed_control_handshakes_total{outcome="rejected_no_application_protocol"}` (TLS alert 120) | delta between two `/metrics` samples per relay, gap 102.3–102.7 s; plus a second independent 103.8 s window (4 rounds) |
| C | Registry authenticity and freshness | HTTPS (GitHub Pages static file), Ed25519 signature over the exact registry payload bytes | two copies (repo vs published) byte-compared + signature verified against the operator key compiled into the client |

**Not measured (stated up front):** no client→relay RTT, no relay→server lag, no
latency of any kind. The relay's `lightspeed_relay_latency_avg_us` is a
relay-wide, unpaired upstream-lag metric (see WF-063) and is not used for any
claim here. **This audit can prove a counter is moving or that a condition holds
in a ~103 s window; it cannot prove a population is gone.** That distinction is
the whole point of §3.

**M1 arithmetic convention.** `ΔM1 = M1(t1) − M1(t0)`; rate = `ΔM1 / gap_s × 3600`.
`accepted = negotiated_v1 + no_alpn` on the same metric family. All counters are
relay-process cumulative, and all eight relays have been up since the 1.11.0
rollout restart on 2026-11-16 14:49–15:05Z, so cumulative values are "since the
flip".

Fleet arithmetic: 390 rejects in 102.9 s (first-to-first) = 13,650/h; the

measurement error.

**Cumulative context since the recorded post-flip snapshot**
(`wat/evidence/2026-11-16/wf067-rollout/fleet-post-flip.json`: M1 = 6,098,
accepted = 17):

- M1 6,098 → 233,924 = **+227,826** in ≈9.56 h ≈ **23,832/h** average.
- accepted 17 → 963 = **+946** ≈ **99/h** average.
- M1 : accepted ≈ **241 : 1**.

**Verdict: M1 is still increasing on 5 of 8 relays, and the drain is not
complete.** Pre-1.11.0 clients are still in the field and still retrying.

**Methodology caveat (the part that matters for task-10).** Accepted handshakes
fleet-wide are ≈1 per minute (99/h), so a ~103 s window normally samples 0–3 of
them; this one sampled **0**. Consequently:

- The three flat relays (lax, nrt, mad) **must not be read as "drained"**: with
  zero accepted handshakes in the window, a flat M1 only says no pre-ALPN client
  happened to retry *there* in those 103 s. The 1.11.0 population was not
  sampled either.
- A clean-window claim needs **accepted ≥ 200 across the window and M1 == 0**
  (plan §7.4). At the current acceptance rate that is a multi-hour, per-relay
  window — not a 100 s sample. Until then, task-10 stays open and no relay can be
  declared clean.
- M1 is a protocol-outcome count, not a latency and not a user count. It also
  cannot say **how many distinct pre-1.11.0 clients** remain: a retry loop can
  produce thousands of rejects from one client. "Flat" would prove the retry
  stream stopped, not that the installed base updated.

**Independent second window (reproducibility).** Four `/metrics` rounds,
00:42:05.761Z → 00:43:49.554Z (103.793 s, `raw/malformed-probe.txt`) reproduced
the rates within ~6%: fleet M1 **+389 ≈ 13,492/h**; per relay ewr 3,815/h,
fra 5,619/h, sgp 971/h, bom 2,116/h, syd 971/h, lax/nrt/mad 0. Accepted in the
204 s from the end of the t1 sample to the last probe round: 963 → 967
(**+4 ≈ 71/h**; sgp +2, fra +2) — consistent with the ~99/h lifetime average and
with 0 in the t0→t1 window being a sampling outcome, not a protocol change.

**Pre-rollout context** (from the published history, `raw/published-network-history.json`):
8/8 relays reported 1.10.3 at 2026-11-16T13:55Z and 1.11.0 at 17:49Z — the
rejects counted above are all post-rollout, on 1.11.0 relays, which is the only
shape they can have at `required`.

"Other drop categories" = `drops_fec_malformed` + `drops_session_setup` +

auth rejections are 54,656 = 99.2% of all 55,097 drops.

**Expected vs concerning:**

- **Auth rejections (99.2% of drops) — expected, not a fault.** A valid tunnel
  header with an unknown/expired session token is rejected, which is the correct
  behaviour. The most consistent explanation is the accepted 1.11.0 breakage: a
  pre-1.11.0 client cannot register at `required`, but a client that was running
  before the relay restart keeps addressing the relay it last used with a dead
  session token. *Interpretation, not proven* — proving it needs a packet capture
  on a relay host, which this audit did not take (and client IPs must not be
  stored per `[PRIVACY_STUB]`).
- **sgp-1 and mad-1 are effectively idle** — 87 and 346 packets relayed in 9.6 h
  (0.002–0.01 pkt/s) while rejecting 7,113 and 8,040. Together with the
  auth-reject ratios this is the strongest per-relay signal of stranded
  pre-1.11.0 clients concentrated on those two relays. Not a safety concern;
  worth re-reading when task-10 closes.
- **Rate limiting (fra only, 409 hits) and abuse blocks (fra 15, mad 15) —
  expected.** fra is the busiest relay (166,867 packets, 35 sessions); 409 hits
  over 9.6 h is a very low absolute rate and no other relay needed the limiter.
  The abuse-detector blocks are in the same class.
- **Egress budget — not enforced anywhere.** `lightspeed_egress_budget_bytes = 0`
  on all eight (0 means "no budget enforced", per the metric's own HELP text), so
  there is no hard egress cap. Traffic is nowhere near a problem (fleet
  133.4 MB in 9.6 h ≈ 3.9 KB/s average; busiest relay bom-1 ≈ 2.5 KB/s), so this
  is informational; it is only a lever if the fleet grows.
- **`drops_malformed` one-off synchronized event — benign, but do not treat the
  counter as a fault indicator.** Between the two samples every relay's
  `drops_malformed` rose by exactly 1 (fra 2→3), and no relay's value changed
  again during the 103.8 s follow-up probe. One packet to each relay in the same
  ~100 s across the whole fleet is the signature of a single internet-wide UDP
  scan/probe sweep, not of fleet traffic. Cumulative malformed drops since
  restart are 0–3 per relay; impact is nil.
- **FEC — 0 recoveries/parity on all relays.** No client used FEC in this window;
  expected for current client behaviour, and it means the fleet's FEC path was
  not exercised by this audit.
- **Updater state — all eight `no_update`, checks 0–61 min before the sample**
  (`last_check_at` 2026-11-16T23:35:58Z–2026-11-17T00:38:39Z), `available_version =
  current_version = 1.11.0`: the fleet is on the latest release by its own
  updater's account. But `rollback_version = 1.11.0` on all eight and
  `last_apply_at` = 2026-11-14 (before the 2026-11-16 deploy) — so
  `/health.update` describes the last *self-updater* apply, not the current
  deploy, and its `rollback_version` is **not** a distinct rollback target.
  Operators must not use it as one (see finding F5).

### 5.2 Freshness — authentic but frozen, and the freshness field is misleading

- **The registry content is 55.7 days old and has not changed since 2026-09-22**
  (the Sydney relay commit `8a998ce`, 2026-09-22 14:45 +07). Nothing in the
  1.11.0 rollout changed a node id, address or key, so re-signing was **not
  required**; discovery is not at risk from this age.
- **`published_at` is wrong as a freshness field.** It reads
  `2026-09-20T08:27:11Z` (57.7 days old at fetch) — 80 s before the previous
  node-set commit (`7130839`, Mumbai + Madrid, 2026-09-20 15:28 +07), i.e. it is
  the *previous* node set's timestamp. The payload also carries
  `issued_at = 2026-09-22T07:44:29Z` (55.7 days old) — 35 s before the Sydney
  commit (`8a998ce`, 2026-09-22 14:45 +07), i.e. the true content timestamp.
  **`published_at` was not bumped when the node set last changed and is 1.97
  days older than `issued_at`.** `issued_at`
  is *not* modelled by the client's `Registry` struct (serde ignores unknown
  fields), so the only freshness field a client actually parses is the stale one.
  No functional harm today (monotonic, verifies, no revoked nodes), but any
  monitor or operator that reads `published_at` as "when this registry was last
  published" will be wrong by two months.
- **`Last-Modified: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 17:50:19 GMT` is the last Pages deploy, not
  registry content.** The hourly Pages workflow re-uploads the whole `web/`
  directory, so the header changes without the registry changing. The scheduled
  workflow is also visibly throttled: last 9 scheduled deploys ran
  2026-11-14T15:28Z, 20:20Z, 2026-11-15T00:39Z, 07:28Z, 15:14Z, 20:44Z,
  2026-11-16T00:39Z, 07:34Z, 17:49Z — gaps of ~4–10 h, not 1 h
  (`raw/pages-workflow-runs.json`, `gh run list --workflow=pages.yml`).
- **Consequence for the site's live stats:** `web/network-history.json` on Pages
  was generated at 2026-11-16T17:49:46Z — **6.8 h stale at audit time**, last
  snapshot 8/8 relays on 1.11.0. The registry file itself is unaffected (static,
  committed), but "the Pages site is fresh" cannot be inferred from a deploy
  having happened.
- `placement.json` is not on the public artifact (HTTP 404) — by design per
  `pages.yml` ("stays off the public Pages artifact").

## 8. Unverified and limits

- **Window length.** M1 is measured over ~103 s (and a second ~104 s window);
  accepted handshakes are ~1/min fleet-wide, so a single window cannot establish
  absence of a population. Flatness on lax/nrt/mad is **not** a clean result.
- **`auth_rejected` cause** is inferred from the counter definition and the
  rollout history, not from packet capture; no relay host access was used and
  client IPs are not stored.
- **Registry identity binding.** Signature/fields verified; the live QUIC
  certificate fingerprints and node pubkeys were not (no client build; `cargo`
  forbidden by the task; data plane is UDP QUIC on :4434).
- **Data-plane reachability** of each `data_addr` was not tested (would need a
  client/QUIC handshake).
- The repo's own `cargo test -p lightspeed-client` registry test was not run
  (task forbids cargo); the independent Node Ed25519 verification replaces it for
  authenticity. `issued_at` is informational only: it is now modelled by
  `Registry` (preserved on re-sign) but deliberately not read by any client
  logic; `published_at` is a monotonic anti-rollback floor, not an expiry.
- `placement.json` (stats branch, advisory) is not published on Pages (404) by
  design; it was not audited further.
